What the chain says

This was not a random retail launch found through a dust-holder list. Stacc’s 7ihN deployed the devnet AMM; Dylan’s 745x signed its Create path the following day.

A year later, 745x reappeared as a user identity in the same Eymp experiment that 5ph / neveruseme minted and moved into Squads. Squads then deliberately allocated 37.723 billion Eymp tokens to 8oqr. Six weeks later, 8oqr used LetsBonk’s production launch path.

What the chain cannot say

It cannot show who physically held the key, what they observed, whether findings were communicated to Pump, or which product, incentive, liquidity or routing decision followed.

It also cannot prove Dylan controlled 8xwk or 8oqr. The report distinguishes user-supplied identity claims for 745x and 5ph from the on-chain acts those addresses performed.

Bottom line

The record starts with Stacc’s devnet AMM, follows Dylan’s tester into the mainnet PUMP hook experiments, crosses a one-person Squads treasury into a purposefully provisioned burner, and ends inside LetsBonk with “titty” / TEST. Pump retook the daily launch count roughly one day later. The full operational trail is documented; the playbook between probe and reversal remains missing.
